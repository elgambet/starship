# Starship HLS interior: what the images actually establish

Research update: 15 November 2026. **No public, dimensioned, deck-by-deck plan for the current Starship HLS cabin was found.** The released views show selected spaces, not a continuous tour of all floors. This page separates photographs and SpaceX concept images from independent proposals. See [IMAGE_SOURCES.md](IMAGE_SOURCES.md) for file provenance.

| Space or function | Public evidence | What remains unknown |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Window-facing control area | SpaceX's [official cabin image](screenshots/spacex-hls-cabin-concept.jpg) and [overhead view](screenshots/spacex-hls-cockpit-view-2-republished.jpg) show seats, screens, windows, a partial deck and central access opening. A [2026 NASA presentation](screenshots/nasa-2026-hls-cabin-progress-slide.png) republishes the overhead concept as an artist's illustration. | Exact deck elevation, room boundary, window and seat dimensions. These are concept renders. |
| Tall central cabin volume | A [SpaceX concept view](screenshots/spacex-hls-cockpit-view-3-republished.jpg) looks upward toward the nose/docking region. | Whether the rendered void, handholds, platform geometry and openings match flight hardware. This is another view of the crew-cabin volume, not proof of a separate floor. |
| Sleeping quarters | A [2022 NASA HLS capability slide](screenshots/nasa-2022-hls-habitability-slide.png) explicitly lists sleeping/eating/hygiene. A [2026 NASA/SpaceX Cabin 1 frame](screenshots/nasa-spacex-cabin-1-2026-main.webp) shows a padded, berth-like space with straps. | NASA/SpaceX have not publicly labeled that frame as a finished sleeping room or released a bunk count, dimensions or location in the full ship. |
| Two airlocks | [SpaceX's HLS update](https://www.spacex.com/updates) specifies **two** airlocks of about **13 m³ each**. A [republished SpaceX airlock view](screenshots/spacex-hls-airlock-view-republished.jpg), a [2022 NASA slide with an overhead mockup photo](screenshots/nasa-2022-hls-airlock-overhead.png), and a [2025 NASA paper photograph of a full-scale mockup](screenshots/nasa-2025-airlock-test.png) show partial physical details. | No public drawing places both airlocks within the deck or gives their linear dimensions. The mockups and republished image are not current flight-design drawings. |
| Garage and elevator route | A [NASA HLS engineer](https://www.nasa.gov/podcasts/houston-we-have-a-podcast/artemis-iii-paving-the-way-to-lunar-landings/) describes the elevator in a garage area below the cabin and the two airlocks. NASA also published [suited mockup tests](https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/esdmd/artemis-campaign-development-division/human-landing-system-program/nasa-astronauts-practice-next-giant-leap-for-artemis/). | A complete garage floorplan, elevator basket dimensions, rail path and internal passage widths. |
| Dining, hygiene, science/storage | The [NASA capability slide](screenshots/nasa-2022-hls-habitability-slide.png) lists eating, hygiene and science/geology functions. SpaceX reports life-support and crew-system testing. | No public room renders or placement for galley, toilet, science bench or storage deck. |
| Exercise or gym | [NASA 2022 committee minutes](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/heoc-oct-31-nov-1-2022-rev2.pdf) discuss exercise devices in HLS mission planning. An [unofficial 2021 HLS section](screenshots/unofficial-hls-interior-deck-plan-design-freedom.jpg) draws fitness machines near crew pods. | No SpaceX or NASA render confirms a dedicated gym, its floor, equipment, or dimensions. The fan section is an older proposal, not the present layout. |

## Important distinction for the 3D model

The [2021 Design Freedom section](screenshots/unofficial-hls-interior-deck-plan-design-freedom.jpg) is explicitly marked as an unofficial concept. It includes crew pods, a social deck, fitness equipment and a single two-zone EVA airlock; the later SpaceX description states **two separate airlocks**. The [2024 TheSpaceEngineer cutaway](screenshots/unofficial-hls-cabin-cutaway.jpg) provides another possible arrangement, but its 19.4 m³ airlock claim conflicts with SpaceX's later approximately 13 m³ figure. Both can inspire an **explorable hypothetical interior**, with visible `estimated` labels. Neither can define the measured flight arrangement.

For the first Three.js version, model the published relationships and dimensions, then use clearly documented placeholders for sleeping, eating, hygiene, storage and exercise. Avoid presenting the workout area as an official floor or letting old fan layouts override the two-airlock requirement.
